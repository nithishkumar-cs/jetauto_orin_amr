# Isaac Sim Perception Validation — 2026-09-27

## Scope

- Isaac Sim 4.5 ran headless on the Windows/Ubuntu development laptop.
- The Jetson ran the production ROS 2 perception graph.
- The laptop published RGB-D, 2D LiDAR, TF, `/clock`, and wheel odometry.
- This run used Wi-Fi. Wired Ethernet was unavailable and remains untested.
- A generated pedestrian cutout provided the semantic target.

The saved final bag is local validation data and is not committed because it is
124.8 MiB. Analyze it with:

```bash
python3 rosbag/analyze_perception_bag.py \
  /home/nithish/jetauto_isaac_validation_20260927_final
```

## Recorded results

The final bag covers 31.319 s of wall time and 10.000 s of simulation time.
Isaac therefore ran below real time during this workload. `/clock` arrived at
18.69 Hz wall time while its simulation timestamp advanced at approximately
60 Hz. RGB-D was configured for a 0.100 s simulation period but arrived at
approximately 2.6 Hz wall time.

| Topic | Messages | Wall rate | Maximum header gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `/camera/rgb/image_rect_color` | 59 | 2.63 Hz | 0.90 s |
| `/camera/aligned_depth_to_rgb/image_raw` | 59 | 2.61 Hz | 1.00 s |
| `/perception/detections_2d` | 52 | 2.51 Hz | 0.90 s |
| `/perception/detections_3d` | 58 | 2.51 Hz | 1.00 s |
| `/scan` | 70 | 2.96 Hz | 0.10 s |
| `/perception/fused_obstacles` | 52 | 2.37 Hz | 1.00 s |

RGB and aligned depth used matching 640×480 grids. Of 59 recorded messages in
each stream, 58 timestamps matched exactly; each stream had one unmatched
timestamp. The image sequence contained one 0.2 s gap and one long outage. The
3D person observations were separated by 1.0 s across the long outage and the
measured position changed by 0.60 m.

The original camera tracker expired identities after 0.50 s, so the recorded
person camera ID changed from 1 to 4 and the recorded fused ID changed from 11
to 233. This was a configuration failure exposed by the system run, not a
detector classification failure.

## Bag-derived fix and replay

The camera-only `max_unobserved_duration_s` is now 1.25 s. The LiDAR tracker
remains at 0.50 s to avoid retaining short-lived geometric clutter. A unit test
reproduces the measured 1.0 s outage and 0.60 m displacement.

The saved detections and TF were replayed through the actual updated camera
tracking node, and its output plus the saved LiDAR tracks were passed through
the actual fusion node:

| Replayed output | Classified person outputs | Person IDs |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `/validation/camera/tracks` | 50 | `[1]` |
| `/validation/fused_obstacles` | 49 | `[7]` |

The person identity therefore remained stable across the recorded outage after
the fix. The replay also introduced a later chair as a distinct track, so the
stable person result was not caused by collapsing every classification into one
identity.

## Known limits

- The recording proves that image timestamps are missing, but it cannot alone
  distinguish an Isaac render/publish interruption from Wi-Fi/DDS loss because
  no simultaneous publisher-side bag was captured on the laptop.
- Ethernet latency and loss still require an A/B test when a cable is available.
- The final bag contains an average of roughly 24.5 LiDAR tracks per message.
  These are mostly unclassified scene geometry. Clustering and association
  thresholds still need scene-based tuning before treating every LiDAR track as
  a useful obstacle identity.
- Camera/LiDAR extrinsics and stopping distances remain simulation values until
  physical hardware is mounted and calibrated.
